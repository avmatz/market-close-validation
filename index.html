An example for solo makers who group one-of-a-kind products by price tier. Count what went out and came home, account for non-sale items, then compare the stock record with the money recorded.

This is a research example, not a finished product or proof of demand. The page does not upload or save your entries. The proposed format is a spreadsheet and printable tally sheet; this browser page only demonstrates the workflow.

    For the researcher: recruitment, answer key and decision rules

    Run a five-vendor comparison, not a product launch

    Find five independent vendors who sell one-of-a-kind pieces at in-person markets. Prefer people who have already done a market. This is a convenience sample for learning, not a statistically representative demand study.

    Ask for a 5-10 minute asynchronous test, or observe one short session if possible. Recruitment can take longer. Do not send bulk messages or post research requests where community rules prohibit them; obtain moderator permission when needed.

    Copy-ready invitation

    Sharing status: this is a public GitHub Pages demo with public source code, not a private study portal. GitHub may record standard access logs, but this page does not submit form entries. Only fictional example data and an empty response tracker are published. Do not enter sensitive information.

    Recruitment restriction: r/CraftFairs currently prohibits promotional posts and AI content. Do not post this AI-assisted concept there. Use existing vendor contacts or a community that explicitly permits this kind of research; obtain approval where needed. A request on this project's own GitHub issue board is not evidence that vendors have seen or joined the test.

    Keep the test neutral

    1. Ask about the last actual event before explaining your proposed solution. Do not pitch its benefits or tell people which answer you want.
    2. Let participants attempt the task before helping. Record observed help separately from self-reported help.
    3. Show the alternatives fairly, including their reviews and the option to keep doing what they already do. The feature-rich DearmoraCo option is included so we do not compare only with simpler products.
    4. Record why they choose something. A polite compliment or a hypothetical US$12 choice is not a sale.

    Answer key for the fictional exercise

    Sample A: 32 units sold, 3 non-sale units, $530 expected and recorded takings, $180 material cost including non-sale units, $249 surplus and $27.67 per entered hour.

    Sample B: recorded takings are $10 below the stock-implied amount; provisional surplus is $239. Raising the booth fee from $60 to $70 reduces it to $229. A good response considers counts, recorded discounts, cash float or payment records rather than silently changing figures to match.

    Screen before spending more time

    Use a practical screening rule: continue to a tiny paid pilot only if at least three of five can complete and explain the task without help, and at least two choose this concept at the stated price and describe a concrete advantage over the alternatives. If the test is asynchronous, label completion as self-reported, not observed.

    This threshold is a decision aid, not proof of market demand. A next-stage pilot still needs actual payment and real use of a standard, non-custom product. Scalability then needs a repeatable acquisition channel that fits the available time; two friendly pilot buyers do not establish that.

    Stop or change direction if vendors prefer free or existing options and cannot identify a meaningful problem we solve better. Do not manufacture a niche to rescue the idea.
